It took me five hours to find my new apartment in the fields of nothing strewn across Iowa.

All of my new friends knew I was lost, constantly perusing my thoughts for useful information between the lines of:

  1. erotic fanfiction
  2. ruminations on bad lunches I had made in sixth grade when
    1. I wanted to become a professional chef before
    2. I wanted to become an unprofessional chef before
    3. I wanted to merely eat food.

Eventually, I followed a flock of vultures past some roadkill on the side of the road, through a ditch, and finally across a field of small budding flowers to find a skinny grain silo with a brownstone apartment attached at the hip. I thanked the vultures, wishing I had some small animal I could sacrifice to them. They bowed deeply and flew off in a synchronous dance to fill their small bird bellies with meals to go. I guess they have the same landlord as me, I thought.

There was no driveway so I found a small patch of naked dirt about the size of a coffee table and expanded it until it resembled my Subaru, transplanting little flowers of various colors and levels of pollen to the outsides of my new car park, while saving a couple for decoration. I decided they would look best on my windowsill so that when I looked out the window it would look like there were mutated giant flowers among the smaller ones. I would laugh and hopefully this dull, immature thought would provide ample entertainment for the Angels as well. Perhaps they’d even let me play games online with them. I was never a big fan of the Call of Duty series but maybe I was just playing with the wrong people. They would yell slurs of all kinds from the couch as I sunk back further into the cushions, cocooned in a large blanket and barely able to breathe.
