The highlight of my time spent in Iowa at that point was watching a pig get slaughtered through the slits of my fingers.

I hid my face as the blood was drained down the stone grate in my basement where the farmers came once a month for that purpose and that purpose alone. They had knocked on my door at around 4am and invited me downstairs, asking if I had ever seen an animal die before. I admitted that I had run over a couple of birds one time with my car but they didn’t seem to think that counted, laughing it off as a ridiculous and inane suggestion as they ground a knife against a whetstone, the noise searing into my ears. I pulled my pajamas tight against my body, afraid of what I was about to witness. Then I remembered that the devil was always watching, as outlined in section 565D of my lease, and I let my body loosen, hands dropping to my side. I wanted to make him proud. I wanted to make him know I was not afraid of him. Blood spattered across my body as the farmers drew the knife along the pigs neck, slicing it open like postage. I let out an unconscious gasp before catching it with my hands and swallowing it back into my lungs. The pig was then hung up to drain and I was given a pint of blood to water the flowers. I took it and thanked the farmers, asking what I should do with the pig.

“Oh, he’ll be here for that in three to six business days.” My heart began to race. Him? Here? I had to start cleaning.

Day Happenings
Day One The first day I ran a continuous spray of air freshener out of a bottle of Febreeze so as to not inconvenience the farmers.
Day Two By day two I had already gotten used to the smell.
Day Three By day three the flies that now swarmed my apartment were silent.
Day Four By day four, the liquid that oozed below the basement door ran clear as the Caribbean sea.
Day Five On the fifth day I woke up early to the noise of thrashing emanating from below me. I quickly grabbed my best robe and slung it over my shoulders, tying it in front before smoothing it out with my palms. With no mirror, I imagined myself a forties film noir femme fatale, sensuously descending the stairs, my fingers teasing the bannister as I crept down one foot at a time, each step revealing more of a leg through the slit of my robe. My world was only black and white with high contrast and shadows that extended across sets. I slipped into a pair of Birkenstocks I bought at a thrift store, pulled my wide shoulders together to accentuate the length of my neck, and began down the stairs to the basement. Intense heat scorched the back of my legs as I descended deeper. Sulphur filled the air, choking me from the inside out as if all breath had been plucked from my lungs like a mussel stolen from the sand until my insides felt like they were cooking in oil. I could feel my soles melting against the cement stairs, each step becoming wetter, my feet sticking to the surface before tearing at the goop and wrenching free. Eventually, I took a deep breath and held onto it knowing it could be my last. I removed my shoes and let my feet turn to fire against the concrete. Shoes were only slowing me down. Air was only slowing me down. I reached the door and grabbed the handle. My hand burst into flames as colorful as the flowers which surrounded me at all times, caging me. I pushed forward and with one last effort, I flew through the basement door to find the left hind leg of the pig protruding from the stone drain, its bone bare. My eyes began to water but the tears evaporated upon creation so I wailed dry.
